# Reference allocator — deterministic implementation contract

This is an implementation default to include in the prepared campaign preview, not a claim of an optimal investment allocation. It replaces undefined “let the agents decide size” with one explicit, testable mechanism. Alternative sizing policies can be registered later without restoring old universal caps.

## `funded_weighted_v1`

Inputs are a single consistent venue resource snapshot, current adopted inventory and reservations, qualified entry candidates, a campaign revision and the strategy registry. Each candidate must carry a finite nonnegative desired allocation weight, estimated net economics, a worst permitted entry price, applicable exit/protection protocol, and versioned evidence. The weight is generated by that strategy's registered deterministic function, never by free-form model text. An experimental strategy can use equal weight among its qualifying opportunities; that is a declared heuristic, not a calibrated probability.

1. Filter candidates by actual instrument/account/session/cost/strategy conditions. Do not fetch the network while holding the reservation transaction. Reject unknowns with a scoped reason; a zero positive-edge candidate set produces NO_NET_EDGE only when the inputs were actually available.
2. Coalesce candidates for the same economic position according to the registered strategy-conflict policy. Default: one candidate per venue/instrument, higher predeclared strategy priority wins, then creation time and candidate ID break ties. Do not add their risk allowances together.
3. Work from **eligible unreserved local cash**, not total holdings, margin buying power, cash at the other venue, or proceeds the account cannot yet reuse. Fees and known exit-cash requirements are included in the funding calculation.
4. Allocate that free cash in proportion to the positive desired weights. If only one eligible candidate has positive weight, all eligible free cash may be considered for it in full-loss mode. This permits concentration; it does not require a candidate to request it. A selected campaign reserve, if any, must be explicit in the sizing-policy parameters.
5. For each candidate in stable priority order, compute quantity as the minimum of its weighted cash allowance, qualified liquidity/impact capacity, valid exit capacity, strategy-specific desired notional, and any enabled campaign risk constraints. In full-loss mode there is no inherited percentage-risk term. Round down to the venue quantity increment, re-evaluate fees and minimum viable entry/exit size, then atomically reserve.
6. Do not redistribute rounding leftovers recursively until they are consumed. Emit the next normal evaluation on a real resource/market change. Dust and minimum-order remainders are not failures to maximize deployment.
7. Existing inventory is managed by its registered exit/scale-in policy. This allocator is not a command to liquidate every position to match fresh weights. A rebalance must itself pass an after-cost change-of-exposure test and a qualified close protocol. Releasing cash triggers reevaluation, not forced churn.

If a strategy omits desired weights or requested exposure, it is not executable under this policy; attach a registry default before the campaign is prepared, not an ad hoc LLM guess per tick. `qty_hint` from the legacy strategy may still act as a reducing upper bound; it cannot enlarge funded quantity.

## Small deterministic test fixture

Eligible free cash is a fictional $100. Candidates A and B have declared weights 3 and 1. Their initial cash allowances are $75 and $25, **including entry fees**, before price/quantity rounding, liquidity and optional selected constraints. With a single candidate A, its ceiling is $100 including fees, rather than the former $18 or $35 ceiling. If A is not economically or mechanically qualified, its existence must not cause an order. These are resource-allocation examples, not instrument recommendations.

## Required builder evidence

Test proportional weights, zero/negative/NaN weights, deterministic ties, multiple proposals for one instrument, fee buffers, fractional rounding, optional selected risk gates, concurrent reservations, and no rebalancing solely because a chat agent changed its wording. Report which registered strategy supplies weights and evidence. Do not describe this reference heuristic as scientifically proven or a money-making edge.
